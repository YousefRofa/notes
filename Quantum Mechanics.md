The main difference between classical and quantum mechanics is that, in a classical world, the relationship between the state of a system and the result of measuring it so trivial, its so trivial that we label them the same way, the momentum of an object and the result of measuring the momentum of the object are the same thing (duh?).

In Quantum mechanics that is not the case, states and measurements are two different things.

All books start by studying the spin of a system since its the most basic, yet the most quantum of all systems. Any attempt to visualize what a spin is however (Like using an arrow in space pointing to a direction) will badly miss the point.

Now, spin is an intrinsic property of a system, denoted by $\sigma$. This spin seems to be a thing due to the Stern-Gerlach experiment that took place in 1922.

The experiment consists of an oven that produced a beam of neutral atoms (Silver atoms), and it was observed that this beam of atoms splits when it passes through a magnetic field. Roughly have the atoms go upwards and the other hald goes downwards with an equal magnitude of deflection when the magnetic field is applied vertically.
